Stochastic reaction networks govern noise-driven dynamics in systems ranging from gene regulation to cellular signalling and population processes. Yet their high dimensionality and sensitivity to initial states obscure transient mechanisms and limit reliable inference from experimental data. Here, we show that stochastic reaction networks in biological systems often exhibit dominant low-dimensional spectral structure that shapes their dynamics. We develop a spectral framework based on the stochastic Koopman operator that identifies these dominant dynamical modes directly from simulated trajectories, yielding reduced representations with computable error bounds. This reduction enables accurate analytical descriptions of the time evolution of moments, parameter sensitivities, and cross-spectral densities, while achieving order-of-magnitude computational gains relative to standard simulation-based methods. Applied to synthetic gene circuits, our framework reveals the spectral organisation of stochastic oscillators, enables systematic comparison of competing intracellular controllers, and facilitates inference of initial-state distributions from single-cell snapshot population data collected over time. Together, these results establish a principled spectral description of stochastic reaction dynamics and provide a foundation for analysing and inferring high-dimensional noise-driven biological systems.

Time series analytics has been widely applied across various domains. While existing time series foundation models (TSFMs) have demonstrated promising results, they still face two major challenges: 1) Most TSFMs mainly rely on stacking Transformer layers to increase parameter size, lacking the ability to adaptively model complex time series patterns from multi-domain data. 2) Current TSFMs are primarily pre-trained for specific tasks, e.g., forecasting or anomaly detection, and a few models support multiple tasks, but they fail to fully leverage task specificity and commonality, resulting in suboptimal performance. To address these challenges, we propose TimeSeek, a novel generalized time series analytics model that adaptively models time series patterns from multi-domain data and unifies multiple tasks in pre-training. From the perspective of the diversity of tasks, we propose multi-task collaborative training, which leverages task specificity and task commonality through task customization and cross-task training, effectively unifying multiple tasks in pre-training and better adapting to diverse downstream scenarios. After pre-training on large-scale time series data, TimeSeek achieves state-of-the-art performance in forecasting, anomaly detection, and classification tasks across both few-shot learning and zero-shot situations, together with favorable inference efficiency.

Cancer cell identity and transitions among states reflect oncogenic mechanisms and predict clinical outcomes. These processes are typically defined through molecular profiling, yet such approaches remain limited in scalability and real-time monitoring. In contrast, nuclear morphology provides a low-dimensional state space that captures cellular identity and dynamic transitions. Using neuroblastoma as a model system, we establish a morphology-based framework that maps individual cells within this state space and tracks their transitions as continuous trajectories. Morphology-defined states show cell line-level concordance with transcriptomic classifications and, at the single-cell level, correspond to molecular and signaling features measured by immunofluorescence. Background Transposable elements (TEs) are increasingly recognized as regulators of gene expression and cellular identity. Single-cell RNA-sequencing (scRNA-seq) enables the analysis of their transcription at cellular resolution, but the repetitive nature of TEs and their frequent overlap with genes create substantial mapping ambiguity. Although several tools quantify TE expression, few support locus-specific analysis, and their performance in single-cell data has not been systematically evaluated. Results We present a comprehensive, scalable benchmarking framework written in Snakemake for locus-level TE quantification in short-read scRNA-seq data, combining real datasets with simulations that provide read-level ground truth. Our simulations show that sequence-diverged insertions can be quantified with relatively high accuracy, whereas young TEs remain difficult to resolve due to unreliable assignment of multi-mapping reads. We observe pronounced family-specific biases and identify gene-TE disambiguation as a major unresolved challenge. Across the evaluated methods, incorporating multi-mapping reads generally increased false-positive calls without substantially improving locus-level accuracy. When loci of all ages were analyzed together, SoloTE in unique-mapper mode achieved the highest detection accuracy. Conclusions Our benchmark delineates the fundamental limits imposed by short-read scRNA-seq on locus-specific TE quantification, providing practical guidance for prospective users. Suggested best practices include focusing locus-level analyses on older insertions, applying unique-mapper strategies to improve precision, aggregating counts at the subfamily level for young TEs, and explicitly checking for gene-TE overlaps. Our workflow is reproducible and extensible, providing a foundation for evaluating emerging methods aimed at resolving TE transcription at single-locus resolution.

Contemporary single-cell RNA-sequencing studies typically use RNA velocity analyses to investigate cellular dynamics. Commonly, RNA velocities are assessed with two-dimensional projections. Although it is well established that nonlinear embeddings of gene expression data cannot comprehensively preserve the structure of the original high-dimensional data across scales, there is no tool that systematically assesses and quantifies the faithfulness of velocity embeddings. This situation potentially leads to misleading velocity visualizations and putative cell state transitions. To this end, we introduce velotest, a cell-level statistical test that quantifies the faithfulness of a velocity embedding with respect to its high-dimensional counterpart. Whether language and mathematics rely on shared or distinct neural representations remains unresolved in cognitive neuroscience. Here, we combined latent features from a large language model with functional magnetic resonance imaging encoding models to test cross-domain generalization in 32 participants performing sentence comprehension and calculation tasks.
