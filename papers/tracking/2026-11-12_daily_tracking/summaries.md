## 1. Chiral magnon Kerr interaction from linear and nonlinear Meissner screening in ferromagnet--superconductor bilayers

- Authors: Hanchen Wang, Tao Yu
- Source hits: arxiv

- Matched groups: N/A

- Topic keywords: N/A
- Journal/source: arxiv
- Publication date: 2026-11-10
- Article: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13937v1

Magnon Kerr interaction showing a dependence of magnon dispersion frequency on its population is a useful functionality for nonlinear and quantum magnonics; its strength and sign depend on magnetic anisotropy, bias, and mode geometry. We here propose to use a superconducting gate to tune the Kerr interaction of magnons in ferromagnetic insulators. Even when the superconductor responds linearly, the frequency shift induced by its reflected dipolar field depends on the longitudinal magnetization. The decrease of this magnetization with increasing precession amplitude therefore produces a Kerr contribution. We go beyond this linear Meissner-screening regime by showing that at finite excitation amplitude, the significant Meissner screening currents reduce the superfluid stiffness, thus enhancing the effective magnetic-field penetration and rendering the Meissner backaction amplitude dependent. This superflow-induced pair breaking adds a second Kerr contribution, controlled by the interfacial superflow relative to the nonlinear screening scale. For yttrium iron garnet gated by dirty niobium, we predict a significant renormalization of the Kerr effect by superconductors, with the resulting Kerr contribution strongly chiral in the Damon--Eshbach configuration. By showing that the linear and nonlinear Meissner screening to the Kerr interaction can be distinguished by varying temperature, our work establishes a route to directional control of magnon nonlinear interactions.

Scientific computing is under increasing pressure to adapt to HPC hardware that is shaped by AI workloads. In these modern ecosystems, extracting reasonable performance without investing major engineering effort is a central issue for domain scientists. Correctness constraints and intricate reference implementations represent further impediments to development. Instead of using a double-precision implementation as a reference, considering the underlying scientific problem directly opens more opportunities for performance improvements when updating and rewriting applications.
  We take seven different portability frameworks---covering pragma-based, DSL, and data-centric approaches---and evaluate them on a microphysics kernel of the ICON climate model. Our performance benchmarks show that each different HPC system benefits from a different combination of optimizations, so we develop the measure of framework expressivity to gauge how easily each framework can customize portable implementations in this way.
